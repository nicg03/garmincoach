First setup

Bring your Garmin data here

Install a small browser extension. It uses the Garmin session you already have — no Python, no terminal, no extra login. After that it updates on its own.

  1. 1
    Add the extension to Chrome

    Download the zip, unzip it, then in Chrome open chrome://extensions, turn on Developer mode, and click Load unpacked. Choose the unzipped folder.

    Download the extension →
  2. 2
    Connect it to this site

    Open Garmin Connect in a tab and sign in if asked. Click the extension icon, paste this address, then approve the connection in the tab that opens.

    …
  3. 3
    Wait for the first days to land

    Keep Garmin Connect open and signed in. The last month arrives in about a minute, and this page fills in by itself. After that the extension refreshes every few hours — or click Sync now on the extension icon.

When this page shows charts, you’re done.

Form (TSB)

CTL minus ATL. Positive means you're fresh; negative means fatigue is ahead of fitness. A dip to −10/−20 in a build is normal. If it stays under −20 and HRV is also down, ease off.

CTL / ATL / load ratio

CTL is ~42-day fitness, ATL ~7-day fatigue. The load ratio is ATL/CTL (ACWR). About 0.8–1.3 is productive. Above 1.5 is where niggles cluster. We cap planned weeks at 1.25.

HRV and resting HR

Only the gap to your baseline matters. HRV down and resting HR up for two or three mornings is the real signal. One odd night is noise.

Sleep score

Garmin's 0–100 blend of duration and stages. Use it with HRV, not instead of it. A short night before a quality day is a reason to swap, not to hero through intervals.

Load and form

Bars are each day's training load. ATL is the last week of fatigue, CTL the last six weeks of fitness, and form is what's left over: below zero you're accumulating, above zero you're fresh.

Recovery

Each line against its own trailing baseline, because the absolute numbers only mean something relative to your normal.

Sleep

Stacked bars are hours in deep, light and REM. The line is Garmin’s sleep score. Consecutive low scores with rising resting HR are a better rest cue than one short night.

Weekly volume

Hours by sport, plus weekly load. Ramp more than about 8% week to week and the next block gets harder than it looks on paper.

Yesterday's load against this morning's HRV

One point per day. A cloud drifting down to the right means the hard days are costing you more than they're giving.

VDOT

          
Jack Daniels’ index of current race fitness, from your best
            recent 5k–half. Training paces (E/M/T/I/R) are percentages of
            this number, not of the time you wish you could run.

        

        

          
80/20

          
Share of running time in HR zones 1–2 vs 3–5 over six weeks.
            Polarised training sits near 80% easy. If hard is winning, the
            easy days are not easy enough.

        

        

          
Foster monotony

          
Mean daily load divided by its standard deviation over 7 days.
            Above ~2 the week is too samey — strain (monotony × total load)
            climbs even if no single day looks huge.

        

        

          
Decoupling

          
How much second-half pace fades vs the first at similar HR.
            Aerobic decoupling over ~5% on a long run means the duration
            outran your current endurance.

        

      

      

        
Personal records

        

      

      

        
Pace curve

        
Best pace at each standard mark.

        

      

      

        
Race predictions

        
VDOT / Daniels, Riegel from your nearest PR, and
          Garmin’s own prediction when we have it.

        

      

      

        
Training paces

        
Daniels zones in min/km. Easy is a range (59–74%
          VO2). T, I and R are narrow bands around the table values. The
          planner stamps these onto every structured workout.

        

      

      

        
Aerobic efficiency

        
Heart rate at easy pace over time. Down and to the
          right is getting fitter, not just running more.
